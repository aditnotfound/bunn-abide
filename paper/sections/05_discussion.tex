\section{Discussion}

% Claims C03-C10
The proposed BuNN advantage did not carry over to this ABIDE-I
functional-connectome pipeline. Learned bundle transport did not improve the
primary performance-density curve over GCN, exceed the strongest regularized
connectome baseline, or preserve matched-anchor effective rank more strongly.
Because the backbone, inputs, tuning budget, and capacity controls were held
fixed, these negative findings speak directly to propagation rather than to
differences elsewhere in the models.

\subsection{Why transport may not help in this setting}

This task differs from the settings that motivate bundle transport in two
important ways. Functional-connectome graphs remain dense after thresholding,
and each node carries its full connectivity row. Every node already has global
information before propagation. Diffusion may be averaging informative global
profiles rather than delivering missing long-range context. The declining
density curves are consistent with aggregation degradation, but they cannot by
themselves establish a general over-smoothing mechanism.

\subsection{Importance of the learned-local control}

BuNN adds parameters through its learned map generator. Comparing it with GCN
alone could mistake added node-wise capacity for an effect of transport. The
learned-local control kept that capacity but removed inter-node diffusion. Its
high raw effective rank shows how an unaligned or unmatched rank curve could
give a misleading impression of preservation. Anchoring each operator to its
matched density-zero control isolates the change introduced by inter-node
transport.

\subsection{Heterogeneity and uncertainty}

The primary BuNN-GCN interval crossed zero, and participant weighting reversed
the point-estimate sign. Leaving out individual sites and changing final seeds
also altered the magnitude, and sometimes the direction, of this small
contrast. The sensitivity analyses show that the average difference is not
stable relative to site and optimization variation. By comparison, the
BuNN-versus-elastic-net contrast and the matched-anchor effective-rank result
kept their direction under every leave-one-site exclusion.

\subsection{Limitations}

The result is conditional on ABIDE-I, C-PAC filtered data without global-signal
regression, the AAL-116 atlas, Pearson correlations with Fisher
transformation, positive-edge thresholding, connectivity-profile node
features, the fixed density grid, the shared backbone, and the equal tuning
budget. Functional correlations do not identify anatomy or causal flow. No
locked external cohort was available, and site sample sizes differed
substantially. Runtime depends on both implementation and hardware.
Representation diagnostics describe computational behavior, not a causal link
between representation change and prediction.

\subsection{Future work}

A useful next step is to lock the full ABIDE-I pipeline and evaluate it on a
preprocessing-compatible ABIDE-II cohort. Separate pre-registered studies
could examine global-signal regression, negative-edge handling, a different
atlas, or node features that do not encode the full connectome. Keeping those
questions separate would protect the present confirmatory result from
post-hoc dataset or diagnosis selection.
