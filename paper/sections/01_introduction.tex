\section{Introduction}

Resting-state functional connectomes summarize statistical associations among
regional blood-oxygen-level-dependent time series. Representing brain regions
as nodes and their functional associations as edges makes graph learning seem
like a natural choice. The usual node feature, however, is an entire row of the
connectivity matrix. Each node begins with information about the whole
connectome. Aggregating neighboring rows may blur useful differences rather
than supply missing context.

A recent benchmark across several datasets found that classical models and
non-graph neural networks can match or outperform graph deep-learning models
on functional-connectome tasks. Its ablations identified message aggregation
as one possible source of degradation \cite{han2026rethinking}. A graph
representation alone is not enough to justify propagation. The operator must
earn its place against identity propagation and strong regularized baselines
evaluated on the same splits.

BuNNs first transport features between node-specific frames using learned
orthogonal maps and then diffuse the aligned features
\cite{bamberger2025bundle}. Theory and synthetic experiments suggest that
this construction can resist representation collapse more effectively than
ordinary graph diffusion. Neural-sheaf models provide related evidence that
non-trivial transport changes diffusion behavior \cite{bodnar2022sheaf}.
Dense functional connectomes with globally informed node features present a
different test.

The project initially treated negative functional correlations as a possible
form of heterophilic message passing. That interpretation did not survive
formalization. Correlation sign does not identify node-label heterophily,
neural excitation or inhibition, anatomy, or causal flow, and it can change
with preprocessing choices such as global-signal regression
\cite{murphy2017global}. We kept the dataset, connectome representation, BuNN
architecture, and original aggregation problem, but shifted the hypothesis to
the behavior of the propagation operator itself.

We ask whether learned bundle transport yields a better held-out-site
performance-density curve than GCN aggregation. Any advantage must also exceed
identity propagation, the learned-local capacity control, and a regularized
connectome baseline. We separately test whether the predictive pattern
co-occurs with representation preservation measured in a common frame. The
scope is one pre-specified ABIDE-I pipeline and one architectural hypothesis;
biological geometry and clinical diagnosis lie outside it.
