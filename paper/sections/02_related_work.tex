\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Connectome prediction.}
ABIDE-I aggregated resting-state fMRI and phenotypic data across international
sites to support large-scale autism research \cite{dimartino2014abide}. The
Preprocessed Connectomes Project subsequently released derivatives from
several pipelines, including C-PAC \cite{abidepcp}. Multi-site prediction is
difficult because acquisition, motion, demographics, and preprocessing vary
by site. Holding out whole sites tests a stricter form of generalization than a
random participant split.

\paragraph{Graph aggregation.}
GCNs apply normalized neighborhood aggregation followed by learned feature
transformation \cite{kipf2017gcn}. Repeated diffusion can contract node
representations, although the practical effect depends on architecture,
features, graph construction, and task. In functional connectomes, the problem
matters because a connectivity-row node feature already describes the whole
graph. The findings of Han et al.\ \cite{han2026rethinking} make a no-propagation control
essential. A comparison among GNN families alone cannot show that aggregation
helped.

\paragraph{Learned transport.}
Neural sheaf diffusion assigns vector spaces and learned maps to graph
incidences, allowing non-trivial transport before aggregation
\cite{bodnar2022sheaf}. BuNN specializes this idea to flat vector bundles with
orthogonal node maps and diffusion-based propagation
\cite{bamberger2025bundle}. Raw embeddings from different node frames cannot
be compared directly. Collapse diagnostics must either place them in a common
frame or use a gauge-invariant quantity. We also include a learned-local
control so that node-wise map capacity is not mistaken for an effect of
inter-node transport.
